片段 3：曲子分析 —— key、form、和弦功能
===================================================================

Take the A Train is a fairly simple song to analyze, as you will see in this
video. whenever you start analyzing a song or especially something like a
standard, then it's important that you start with the big picture and then you
add details while you're zooming in. so the two things you wanna ask yourself
will probably be: what is the key, and what is the form? so in this case the
form is a thirty-two bar AABA, and the key is C major. so since we know that
it's a thirty-two bar AABA, that means that we also really only need to analyze
an A part and a B part, and then we have the whole thing pretty much figured
out.

if we start with the A part, most of the chords are actually just in the key —
so of course this is in the key of C major, and the first chord is just the
tonic chord, the Cmajor7, and then a few bars later we have two other chords
that are also in the key, namely the two-chord Dminor7 and the G, the five-chord.
and now we've already covered most of what is there. the only chord that we need
to think a little bit about is the D — so in this case D is not in C major, but
it's a chord, it's dominant, and it's resolving to the fifth, the other dominant
in C major, so it's a D resolving to a G. so that's called the dominant of the
dominant — that's a very common chord to come across in a major key, or actually
also in a minor key, so you want to be able to recognize that quite quickly, and
in this case since it's a D resolving to a G, which is a major chord, then we
can just treat that as a D in that key, so in G major — that means that we need
a G major scale to improvise over this.

in the second A, before moving to the bridge, we have one chord that's different
from the first A, and that's a C7. but again, the C is here a secondary
dominant that's there to take us to the Fmajor7, which is the four-chord in the
key, and we really just — since it's resolving to a major chord — we're just
going to treat it as a C in the key of F major, and use the F major scale when
we're improvising over it.

in the bridge, there's actually only one chord that we need to analyze because
we've covered most of it already, and that's the first one, the Fmajor7, and
that's just the four-chord in the key, so we're just going to the subdominant.
and then we're going from there to the D7, that's a five-of-five, we already had
that in the A part, and we have a two-five back to the tonic, which is Dminor7
G7, and that's also already been covered. I'm sure you'll agree that this is not
a very complicated song. in fact, we can play most of it just using the C major
scale, and then we have a few spots where we need a G major and an F major
